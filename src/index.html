A polished answer can feel like progress. But if the tool does the remembering, choosing, and checking, your output may improve while your own skill stands still. Here’s how to use AI without handing over the part that helps you learn.

The idea in one sentence

Don’t outsource the reps.

Cranmer’s distinction: AI should accelerate you, not replace you. You remain responsible for the goal, the judgment, and the final check.

“Use AI to accelerate yourself, but not to replace yourself.”

A useful test: could you explain, defend, or reproduce the result without the model?

02 / A practical learning loop

Keep one honest attempt in the loop

The talk points to retrieval practice and “desirable difficulty”: struggling a little can feel worse in the moment while improving what sticks later.

01 — TRYMake a first pass

Work the problem before asking for a solution. Write down where you get stuck.

02 — ASKRequest a hint

Ask for a question, clue, or next step—not the finished answer.

03 — EXPLAINTeach it back

Close the answer and explain the idea in your own words, from memory.

04 — CHECKVerify the claim

Use a test, plot, example, or source. Keep responsibility for the result.

Attempt→Hint→Recall→Verify↺
03 / Try this prompt

Turn the model into a coach

Copy this when you want to learn a topic instead of just getting an answer.

Teach me [topic] by asking one question at a time. Start with a concrete example. Wait for my attempt before giving hints. If I’m stuck, give the smallest useful hint. Don’t reveal the full solution until I’ve tried. Afterward, ask me to explain the idea from memory and give me one new problem. Correct errors clearly, and flag anything you are uncertain about.
04 / Check your understanding

Teach it back

What is the difference between AI helping you learn and AI merely helping you finish?

Answer in one or two sentences. The goal is retrieval, not a perfect phrase.

A useful mental picture

Let the machine widen your reach, not flatten your judgment

The talk also warns that common, default AI use can pull work toward familiar answers. A model can make a draft faster or suggest a path; your taste, questions, and willingness to test an unusual idea are what help the result become yours.

For visual or technical work, ask for a plot, diagram, or tiny experiment. Then inspect it. Seeing a claim lets you challenge it—and testing it gives you evidence that a fluent paragraph can’t.
